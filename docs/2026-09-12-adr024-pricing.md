# ADR 024 amendment — Published prices for Entry / SaaS / Sovereign

- Status: **ACCEPTED** — 2026-09-13 (proposed 2026-09-12). Owner sign-off obtained
  on the numbers below as-is. Resolves G5/#113, the fifth and last hard GA blocker
  named in [ADR 024](2026-09-11-adr-launch-readiness.md). Published at
  `https://onssa.org/pricing.html` — the one page on the distribution with no
  basic-auth gate, so a prospect can see a price without asking.
- Relates to [ADR 002](2026-08-18-adr-commercial-multitenancy.md) ("price by
  concentration × regulation" — decided directionally, never finalized),
  [ADR 015/016](2026-08-30-adr-distribution-three-tier.md) (the three planes),
  [#49](../) (Marketplace packaging, still open).

## Context

ADR 002 committed to a pricing *shape* — concentrated/regulated verticals (banking,
investment-banking, private-markets) command a premium; fragmented ones (fintech,
advisory, entry-tier verticals) are priced low — but explicitly deferred the number:
"Never emit a price as final." ADR 024 named this the last blocker before any tenant
can be sold to. This amendment sets the number.

**What grounds the number, from the repo/account, not from a market survey:**

- **Infra ceiling ~$100/mo** (`CLAUDE.md`, `docs/CONTEXT.md`) covers the *entire*
  shared pipeline + KB + dashboard, serving every SaaS tenant at once. Marginal
  cost of one additional SaaS tenant is **near-zero** — an entitlement row + a
  Cognito user; no new compute, no new ingestion (ADR 002 §2: ingestion is shared
  and wholesale regardless of who's watching).
- **Sovereign / in-account tenants bear their own AWS bill** (ADR 015) — the same
  ~$100/mo-class stack, deployed once per tenant, in their account. Onça's own
  marginal cost per Sovereign tenant is the engineering/support cost, not compute.
- **Entry-tier tenants are the fragmented, high-count, low-ARPU case by design**
  (ADR 015 §"Entry Portal") — thousands of tenants must be viable at near-zero
  per-tenant cost, which caps what Entry can charge structurally, not just
  competitively.
- The GA-readiness table (ADR 024, 2026-09-12 run) is the honest input for *which*
  verticals can be sold as premium today: **banking, fintech, investment-banking**
  are GA-ready; nine more are "adequate" (CSO/CRO depth, thinner CCO); three
  (`closed-pension`, `securitization`, `private-markets`) are **not ready** and
  excluded from sale regardless of tier (#119).

No competitor benchmarking was done for this amendment — that's a real gap (see
Alternatives) but the launch-gate exists to stop analysis from blocking a
sellable price, not to produce the *optimal* one.

## Decision

### Entry — R$ 490/month per tenant, single vertical

- Self-serve plane, entry-tier verticals only (`agri-funds`, `betting`,
  `consorcio`, `crypto`, `real-estate-funds` — `tenant_config.ENTRY_INDUSTRIES`).
- One vertical per tenant, shared portal infrastructure.
- Priced to be an easy "yes" for a fragmented, price-sensitive buyer segment
  (independent consórcio administrators, regional brokers) — the ADR 015 design
  point that per-tenant cost must approach zero holds regardless of what's
  charged, so this tier optimizes for volume, not margin per tenant.
- Deferred from active sales per ADR 024 §"Launch sequence is Design-Partner-first"
  — this price is published so #113 is closed and so Entry can be switched on the
  moment #115 (self-serve funnel) ships, not because Entry sells today.

### SaaS — per-vertical modules, tiered by concentration

Monthly, per tenant, billed per licensed module (vertical). A tenant licensing
multiple verticals pays the sum, discounted 15% from the third module onward.

| Tier | Verticals | R$/month per module |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Premium | banking, investment-banking, private-markets\* | 8,900 |
| Mid | insurance, asset-management, wealth-management, financial-data-analytics, acquiring, agri-funds | 4,900 |
| Entry-in-SaaS | fintech, advisory, betting, crypto, consorcio, real-estate-funds | 2,900 |

\* `private-markets` is currently **not GA-ready** (#119) — listed here for the
pricing *shape* only; it is not sellable until its coverage clears the bar, at
which point this price applies without a separate pricing decision.

Rationale for the spread: banking/investment-banking are both the highest-value
buyer (regulated, well-funded strategy teams) and the deepest today (GA-ready,
CPO maturity 70-84 in the ADR 024 table) — concentration and regulatory intensity
both push the ceiling up, exactly as ADR 002 intended. The mid tier holds verticals
that are "adequate" per ADR 024 (CSO/CRO depth, thinner CCO) — priced below premium
until CCO coverage broadens (#118). `closed-pension` and `securitization` are
excluded from the SaaS price list entirely (#119) — no listed price until they clear
GA readiness.

### Sovereign / in-account — license fee + AWS pass-through

- **R$ 25,000/month platform license fee**, paid to Onça, covering all verticals
  the tenant wants deployed in their own account, unlimited internal seats.
- **Tenant pays their own AWS bill directly** (their account, their invoice) — no
  markup, no pass-through billing relationship for infra. This keeps Onça off the
  hook for a tenant's AWS spend variance and matches how the stack is actually
  deployed (ADR 015: "the entire stack deployed directly into the customer's own
  AWS account").
- This resolves the "per-account fee vs. licence + AWS bill pass-through"
  question #113 posed in favor of **licence, no pass-through** — simpler contract,
  and AWS's own enterprise discount programs already serve large accounts better
  than anything Onça could re-sell.
- Feeds #49 (AWS Marketplace listing): the R$25k/mo figure is the number to submit
  as the Marketplace private-offer price once that packaging work starts.

## Consequences

- A prospect on any plane can now be quoted a number without an ad hoc decision —
  G5 is closed.
- The SaaS per-module structure means a design partner's actual invoice depends on
  which verticals they need, not a single flat "SaaS price" — this must be reflected
  wherever pricing is quoted externally (this doc + `docs/trust/` are internal;
  the actual customer-facing price sheet is a separate deliverable once a design
  partner is being closed).
- Entry's price is published but the plane stays deliberately unsold until #115
  ships, per the existing ADR 024 sequencing decision — this amendment does not
  reopen that sequencing.
- `private-markets`/`closed-pension`/`securitization` staying priceless-until-ready
  keeps the "we sell what is live and cited" discipline (ADR 024 §4) intact —
  publishing a price for a thin sector would be the same honesty failure that rule
  exists to prevent.

## Alternatives considered

- **Flat per-seat SaaS pricing** (common SaaS pattern). Rejected: the buyer's value
  driver here is *sector coverage*, not seat count — a strategy team of 3 gets the
  same corpus value as one of 30. Per-module pricing matches what's actually being
  sold.
- **Usage-based pricing** (per query, per report). Rejected for launch: adds a
  metering dependency (explicitly parked per the user's standing decision to hold
  billing/metering work) and doesn't fit a habit-forming weekly-brief product where
  the value is *coverage*, not *transactions*.
- **Wait for competitor benchmarking before publishing.** Rejected per ADR 024's
  own framing — an enumerable, boundable task (four days of research) was already
  the wrong thing to let block a real prospect conversation. The numbers above are
  a defensible first price, not a permanent one; revisit after 2-3 real
  design-partner conversations surface actual price sensitivity.
